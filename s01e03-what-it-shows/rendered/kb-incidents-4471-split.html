The problems

parser: day-first dates read month first

The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a booking for 7 April goes out as 4 July, and 12 October as 10 December. This is the bug #4471 describes, and it hits every tenant through the app, email and the booking-v2 form.

Fix: parse dates in the tenant’s locale (dayFirst() in causes.ts already asks the platform).

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type month first

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. People type what the hint says, and the date comes out flipped. Only tenants on booking-v3 have it, and only through the form. The month/day swap looks the same as parser, but the parser fix does not touch this form.

Fix: print the tenant’s own order under the field, as booking-v2 does (DD/MM/YYYY).

one-day: dates move by one day, no swap

These reports describe a date that moved by one day: Tuesday 12 May shown as Monday 11 May, “every booking in June is one day off”, a client in Lisbon who got Tuesday while a client in Toronto got Monday, an invite that arrived before the booking was made. A month/day swap cannot produce any of these. The desk had already noted them (desk-notes.json); the #4471 page counted them under parser as a footnote.

Likely cause, not yet confirmed: a date converted through UTC or the viewer’s time zone, so it lands on the day before. Engineering should check how invite dates are stored and rendered.

How these were counted

  • By id. The ticket system exports in pages that overlap, so some reports come twice. Each report is counted once.
  • By tenant and form, not by intake tag. Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser, and that guess often disagrees with the tenant (see causes.ts). The tag is ignored here.
  • By what people describe. A month/day swap and a one-day move are different symptoms, so they are different problems, even when the subject line is the same.

Lessons

  • A subject line tells you what people noticed, not what broke. Merge on cause, not subject.
  • Check a big ticket’s reports against the tenant directory before marking it known.
